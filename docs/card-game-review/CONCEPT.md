# CONCEPT.md — STRATEGIC CARD GAME CONCEPT v0.2

**Status:** Original human-authored concept, provisional.

**Project phase:** Early concept development.

**Creative authority:** Human creators.

**Implementation status:** No programming or playable prototype requested.

## 1. Core Game Concept

The project aims to develop an original two-player strategic card game that can be played both physically and digitally.

Players construct decks, summon fighters to the battlefield, manage resources and develop their fighters during a match.

The central proposed distinguishing feature is a **class-specific evolution system**.

Different fighter classes progress through fundamentally different gameplay actions, giving each class a distinct strategic identity.

Nothing in this document is finalized. Names, mechanics, numerical values, themes and structures may be modified, combined or discarded during development.

## 2. Battlefield Structure

Each player provisionally has:

- One active fighter participating primarily in combat.
- A reserve area containing approximately 3–5 additional fighters.
- A resource area for resource cards.
- A personal draw deck.
- A hand of cards.
- A discard or removal area for destroyed and used cards.

Players may potentially switch fighters between the active position and reserve.

Fighters may potentially evolve both while active and while in reserve.

Evolution requirements may differ between classes.

The exact number of reserve slots and all related rules remain undecided.

## 3. Resource System

The game provisionally uses dedicated resource cards.

An illustrative deck could contain approximately 60 cards, with around one-third being resource cards.

These numbers are examples, not requirements.

Resources may enable:

- Playing fighters.
- Performing attacks.
- Using special abilities.
- Potentially progressing fighter evolution.

The following remain undecided:

- How resources are played.
- How much resource each card produces.
- Whether resources are reusable or consumed.
- Whether resources refresh automatically.
- How many resources can be played per turn.
- Whether different classes interact differently with resources.

The resource system should support meaningful strategic decisions without unnecessary complexity.

## 4. Four Placeholder Fighter Classes

The following four classes are provisional design archetypes.

Their names and mechanics can change.

### RAGE — Power Through Damage

RAGE fighters develop by receiving damage.

The player must balance taking damage against the risk of losing the fighter.

Potential strategic identity:
Risk management, endurance and controlled aggression.

### HOPE — Growth Through Resources

HOPE fighters develop by collecting, spending or controlling resources.

Potential strategic identity:
Planning, resource efficiency and long-term development.

### LUCK — Growth Through Successful Actions

LUCK fighters develop through successful attacks, hits or other successful actions.

Potential strategic identity:
Initiative, calculated risk-taking and momentum.

The exact meaning of success is not yet defined.

### DEATH — Power Through Loss

DEATH fighters develop when fighters are destroyed or sacrificed.

A player may benefit from losing their own fighters.

Potential strategic identity:
Sacrifice, attrition and converting losses into future advantages.

These four classes are starting points, not a finalized class system.

Future design may introduce additional classes, combinations and cross-class synergies.

## 5. Evolution System

Fighters may evolve during a match.

One possible structure consists of three stages:

- Stage I — Base form.
- Stage II — Developed form.
- Stage III — Final form.

Evolution may:

- Improve fighter attributes.
- Unlock new attacks.
- Modify existing abilities.
- Introduce new tactical or strategic possibilities.

Evolution implementation remains open.

Options worth exploring include:

- Dedicated evolution cards.
- A separate evolution reserve or pool.
- Automatic evolution after meeting specific conditions.
- Hybrid systems combining these approaches.

The evolution system should be distinctive, strategically engaging and manageable in physical play.

No specific evolution implementation has been approved.

## 6. Combat System

Each player provisionally has one active fighter at a time.

Active fighters use attacks and abilities, potentially with resource costs.

Players may potentially switch their active fighter with one from reserve.

The following systems have not yet been designed:

- Victory conditions.
- Damage calculation.
- Turn structure.
- Combat resolution.
- Fighter health and durability.
- Switching costs and restrictions.
- Attack timing.
- Defensive interactions.

These are open design questions, not omissions that should automatically be resolved without human input.

## 7. Physical Playability

The game must be fully playable using physical cards and appropriate physical components without requiring software.

Design principles:

- Mechanics should be understandable and manageable.
- Evolution progression should be easy to track.
- Avoid excessive calculation and bookkeeping.
- Physical cards, markers and other components must support the game's essential mechanics.
- The physical version must not depend on a digital companion application.

A future digital version may automate rules and interactions but must not be necessary to play the physical game.

## 8. Creative Development Philosophy

All mechanics remain provisional.

The design process should emphasize:

- Enjoyable gameplay.
- Strategic depth.
- Meaningful player choices.
- Mechanical originality.
- Balance between different fighter classes.
- Accessible physical implementation.
- Clear rules and understandable interactions.

Creative agents must not simply agree with existing ideas.

They should identify weaknesses and propose alternative solutions.

The human creators retain authority over major decisions.

The project must clearly distinguish:

- Original human-authored concepts.
- AI-generated suggestions.
- Experimental design alternatives.
- Unresolved questions.
- Human-approved decisions.

Do not treat agent suggestions as finalized mechanics.

Avoid overdesigning multiple systems simultaneously.

Progress should be iterative and focused.

## 10. Initial Creative Exploration

The original concept proposes beginning the creative discussion with the game's possible world, theme and identity.

Questions include:

- What do players actually control?
- Are the fighters creatures, warriors, monsters, divine beings, machines or something more unusual?
- Why do fighters evolve?
- How could RAGE, HOPE, LUCK and DEATH relate to a coherent fictional world?
- Can the game develop a distinctive identity outside conventional fantasy or science fiction?

Several alternative worlds or themes may be explored.

However, the human creators must participate in selecting any direction.

No theme, worldbuilding or visual identity should be finalized autonomously.

**Current project priority:** Strengthen the core gameplay mechanics before extensive worldbuilding. The original interest in exploring themes should be preserved as a future creative direction, not discarded.
